The Charleston Bachelorette Boat Day

Harbor views, a sandbar stop, your own playlist and a cooler you packed yourselves. Here's how to plan the afternoon most groups call the best part of the weekend.

A day on the water does what a bar crawl can't: it gets the whole group in one place, away from King Street crowds, with the skyline, Fort Sumter and the Ravenel Bridge sliding by while somebody's maid of honor runs the aux. Charleston's harbor, creeks and barrier islands give you three distinct ways to do it — a big party catamaran, a slower sail, or a small boat that drops you on a beach — and the right pick mostly comes down to group size and how loud you want the afternoon to be.

Party Catamarans for Big Groups

If your crew is pushing twenty or more, Charleston Party Cat is the obvious starting point. The original Party Cat was custom built for exactly this, carries up to 40 guests, and is BYOB with a shaded roof, plenty of cooler space and a sound system you control. Pick the two-hour harbor cruise if you just want to dance past downtown, or the three-hour beach bash, which adds an island stop for swimming. Their larger Lux Cat handles up to 45 with two restrooms, and the Karaoke Cat is a smaller option for about 19 people who would rather sing than swim.

For a group in the 10-to-14 range that wants a little more structure, Holy City Boat Tours runs private charters out of Ashley Marina on Lockwood Drive. They price by the boat rather than per person, which makes the split easy, and reviewers regularly mention dock stops at waterfront restaurants on the longer trips. Book early — their prime weekend slots go months out.

Sunset Sails and Slower Afternoons

Not every bride wants a floating dance floor. A catamaran sail swaps the bass for quiet, stable decks and the kind of golden-hour photos that end up framed. Charleston Sailing Charters runs the 55-foot Palmetto Breeze out of Shem Creek, which Mount Pleasant Magazine readers voted both best party boat and best sunset boat, plus Serendipity, an 18-passenger luxury cat that departs downtown. The Palmetto Breeze's Sunday mimosa brunch sail is a smart recovery move after a big Saturday night.

Smaller groups can have a whole sailboat to themselves on OM Sailing Charters, a 43-foot Leopard catamaran docked at Patriots Point. Charters are built for six, with room for up to twelve at a small per-head add-on, and bachelorette groups in their reviews keep calling it the highlight of the trip. For a classic ticketed cruise instead of a private boat, Charleston Harbor Tours runs daily harbor and sunset trips that work well for a mixed-age group or a welcome-night outing.

Tiki Boats and Sandbars Off Folly

Staying on Folly Beach? Skip the drive into town and leave from the Folly Beach boat landing at the foot of the bridge. Folly Tiki Boat runs BYOB thatched-roof cruises through the creeks behind the island, with dolphins almost guaranteed and an adults-only after-sunset trip that suits a bach group perfectly. Out here the draw is landing on an empty stretch of sand, swimming, and cruising by the Morris Island Lighthouse, which you can only really appreciate from the water. Afterward, Bowen's Island Restaurant is the low-key oyster spot locals send everyone to.

Driving Your Own Boat

For a smaller, confident group, renting a deck boat or center console is the cheapest way onto the water and gives you total control of the route. Shem Creek Boat Rentals on Seafood Drive puts you right at the mouth of the harbor, and Coastal Expeditions nearby handles kayaks and paddleboards if half the group wants something calmer. One rule to know: anyone born after July 1, 2007 needs a South Carolina boater education card to drive a motorized rental, and whoever is at the wheel should stay off the drinks. If nobody wants that job, book a captained boat instead.

Know before you go: Shem Creek's public dock gets jammed on summer weekends, and Mount Pleasant has stepped up enforcement against unlicensed charters. Book with an established, Coast Guard–licensed operator, ask exactly where you meet, and bring cash for the crew's tip. Most boats ban red wine and glass, so pack cans.

Where to Tie Up for Drinks and Food

Half the fun of a Shem Creek departure is that the restaurants are built for boats. Red's Ice House started as a shrimp dock and still has a dock bar you can pull right up to, with a rooftop deck for sunset. Next door, Saltwater Cowboys has a big outdoor deck among the shrimp boats, and Water's Edge, which caters the Palmetto Breeze, is the dressier option for a post-sail dinner. Heading toward Isle of Palms instead? The Boathouse at Breach Inlet has its own dock on the Intracoastal.

How to Plan the Day

Book the boat first and build the weekend around it — good captains fill up early, especially April through October. Saturday afternoon is peak demand; a Friday sunset sail or a Sunday brunch cruise is easier to get and often cheaper. Plan on two hours if you just want the harbor, three if you want a beach stop. Coordinate matching swimsuits or captain hats ahead of time, freeze water bottles to double as ice, and keep the rest of the day light. Nobody wants a fancy dinner reservation forty minutes after stepping off a boat.
